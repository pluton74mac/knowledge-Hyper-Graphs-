# The LLM era, 2023–2026

Up to 2023, a knowledge hypergraph was something a *person* or a *pipeline* curated, and the
research question was how to embed or complete it. From 2024 the question changed: a language model
can read a corpus and emit n-ary facts directly, so the hypergraph becomes an artefact of inference
rather than of curation, and the interesting problems move to **construction quality, retrieval, and
evaluation**.

This note traces that shift to 2026-09. Dated one-liners: [timeline.md](timeline.md). Construction
methods in detail: [../03-construction/llm-based-khg-construction.md](../03-construction/llm-based-khg-construction.md)
and [../03-construction/n-ary-relation-extraction-from-text.md](../03-construction/n-ary-relation-extraction-from-text.md).

## 1. The four things LLMs changed

1. **Construction.** Extraction of n-ary facts stops being a supervised IE task with an annotated
   schema and becomes a prompting-and-verification task over raw documents.
2. **Use.** The consumer of the hypergraph is a retriever feeding a generator, not a link-prediction
   scorer. "Hits@10 on a held-out tuple" is replaced by "answer quality on a QA benchmark."
3. **Interface.** The hypergraph must be *serialised into tokens* to be usable by the model, which
   turns representation design into a prompt-engineering problem.
4. **Evaluation.** End-to-end QA metrics hide where the system failed. Whether the hypergraph helped
   at all becomes genuinely hard to establish — and, as §5 shows, is now openly contested.

## 2. 2023–2024: the GraphRAG template

The immediate ancestor is graph-based RAG:

> Edge, D. et al. (Microsoft), "From Local to Global: A Graph RAG Approach to Query-Focused
> Summarization" ([arXiv:2404.16130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130), 24 Apr 2024). The
> implementation was released as open source in **July 2024** and is described by its maintainers as
> "a modular graph-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation system"; as of the check on 2026-09-20 the
> repository states it is "largely in maintenance mode"
> ([github.com/microsoft/graphrag](https://github.com/microsoft/graphrag)).

GraphRAG's recipe — have a model extract entities and relations from chunks, build a graph, detect
communities, summarise them, answer global questions from the summaries — is the template that every
hypergraph RAG system modifies. Its limitation is the one this knowledge base exists for: each edge
connects exactly two entities.

In parallel, the foundation-model idea arrives in KG reasoning: Galkin et al., ULTRA, "Towards
Foundation Models for Knowledge Graph Reasoning"
([arXiv:2310.04562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04562), ICLR 2024) — a single pretrained model that
does zero-shot inference on graphs with unseen entity *and* relation vocabularies. That idea is
transplanted to hypergraphs in 2025 (§4).

## 3. 2025: hypergraph RAG proper

Two systems, three weeks apart, define the area.

### 3.1 HyperGraphRAG (Mar 2025)

Luo, H., Haihong E, Chen, G., Zheng, Y., Wu, X., Guo, Y., Lin, Q., Feng, Y., Kuang, Z., Song, M.,
Zhu, Y., Luu, A. T. "HyperGraphRAG: Retrieval-Augmented Generation via Hypergraph-Structured
Knowledge Representation" ([arXiv:2503.21322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21322), 27 Mar 2025;
**accepted to NeurIPS 2025 main conference**;
[proceedings PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/df55ee6e59f8ac4a625219e11fe9ddba-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

The stated move: "GraphRAG advances [chunk retrieval] by graph-based knowledge representation" but
"remains limited to binary relations"; HyperGraphRAG represents n-ary facts as hyperedges, extracted
by a language model, and retrieves over them. Evaluated in medicine, agriculture, computer science
and law. This is the paper that makes "knowledge hypergraph" a term in the retrieval community
rather than only the embedding community.

### 3.2 Hyper-RAG (Mar 2025 → *Nature Communications* 2026)

Feng, Y., Hu, H., Hou, X., Liu, S., Ying, S., Du, S., Hu, H., Gao, Y. "Hyper-RAG: Combating LLM
Hallucinations using Hypergraph-Driven Retrieval-Augmented Generation"
([arXiv:2504.08758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08758), 30 Mar 2025). Published as *Nature
Communications* **17(1), article 5778, 27 April 2026**
([DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-71411-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-71411-1) — verified via
Crossref on 2026-09-20). Frames the contribution as hallucination reduction in a medical domain,
reports ~12.3% accuracy improvement over the base generator and >6 points over competing retrieval
methods, and ships a lightweight variant with roughly double the retrieval speed.

The *venue* is the notable part: a general-science journal publishing a hypergraph-RAG system is the
clearest signal that the idea left the graph-ML niche.

### 3.3 The rest of 2025

- **Complex query answering moves to hypergraphs.** Tsang, Wang, Song, "Transformers for Complex
  Query Answering over Knowledge Hypergraphs"
  ([arXiv:2504.16537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16537), 23 Apr 2025): new CQA datasets over
  knowledge hypergraphs and a two-stage Logical Knowledge Hypergraph Transformer handling
  projection, negation, conjunction and disjunction.
- **Time.** Un, Lu, Yang, Yang, "VITA: Versatile Time Representation Learning for Temporal
  Hyper-Relational Knowledge Graphs" ([arXiv:2505.11803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11803),
  17 May 2025): one representation covering all four validity types — *since*, *until*, *period*,
  *time-invariant*.
- **Field-level synthesis.** Wei, Guan, Li, Jin, Guo, Cheng, "A Survey of Link Prediction in N-ary
  Knowledge Graphs," EMNLP 2025 (main), pp. 28545–28567
  ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1451/);
  [arXiv:2506.08970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08970), 10 Jun 2025) — the first comprehensive
  survey of the n-ary link-prediction literature.
- **Agentic planning over KHGs.** Zai, Tan, Wang, Liu, Xu, Zhang, "PRoH: Dynamic Planning and
  Reasoning over Knowledge Hypergraphs for Retrieval-Augmented Generation"
  ([arXiv:2510.12434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12434), 14 Oct 2025; **accepted to The Web
  Conference 2026**), which reports surpassing HyperGraphRAG "by an average of 19.73% in F1 and
  8.41% in Generation Evaluation score."

## 4. Hypergraph foundation models (2025–2026)

Two distinct foundation-model programmes, from different communities:

- **Hyper-FM** — Gao, Feng, Liu, Han, Du, Wu, Hu, "Hypergraph Foundation Model"
  ([arXiv:2503.01203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01203), 3 Mar 2025; published *IEEE TPAMI*
  48:4063–4080, April 2026, [DOI 10.1109/TPAMI.2025.3647504](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2025.3647504),
  verified via Crossref 2026-09-20). Cross-domain knowledge extraction from *text-attributed*
  hypergraphs, with 11 curated datasets, ~13.4% average improvement over baselines, and the first
  proposed **scaling law for hypergraph models** — whose headline finding is that *domain diversity*
  matters more than raw vertex/hyperedge count. Treat the scaling-law claim as an active research
  claim, not an established result: it rests on one paper's curated corpus.
- **HYPER** — Huang, Galkin, Bronstein, Ceylan, "HYPER: A Foundation Model for Inductive Link
  Prediction with Knowledge Hypergraphs"
  ([arXiv:2506.12362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12362), 14 Jun 2025). Generalises to *unseen
  entities and unseen relations* of arbitrary arity by encoding each entity together with its
  position in the hyperedge; 16 new inductive datasets. Venue: presented at the NeurIPS 2025 "New
  Perspectives in Graph Machine Learning" workshop ([NeurIPS virtual page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/127653));
  the authors' repository states **ICLR 2026** ([GitHub](https://github.com/HxyScotthuang/HYPER)).
  This is the direct descendant of ULTRA for hypergraphs.

## 5. The critical turn (2025–2026): does the hypergraph actually help?

The most important development of the period is not a system but a set of negative and
qualifying results.

**Graph structure in RAG is not free.** Xiang, Wu, Zhang, Chen, Hong, Huang, Su, "When to use Graphs
in RAG: A Comprehensive Analysis for Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation"
([arXiv:2506.05690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05690), 6 Jun 2025; ICLR 2026;
[GraphRAG-Bench](https://github.com/GraphRAG-Bench/GraphRAG-Benchmark)) starts from the observed
paradox that "GraphRAG frequently underperforms vanilla RAG on many real-world tasks," argues that
existing benchmarks cannot separate the contribution of the graph from that of the corpus, and
supplies a benchmark with stage-specific metrics for construction, retrieval and synthesis. Any
hypergraph-RAG claim should now be reported against a bench of this kind.

**Specialised hyper-relational embedding models may be over-credited.** Wang et al., CIKM 2025
([arXiv:2508.03280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03280)) find classical KG embedding models on
*decomposed* hyper-relational graphs matching specialised models (see
[machine-learning-era.md](machine-learning-era.md) §2.5).

**LLM extraction is the weak link.** Khrouf, Fillastre, Correia, "Optimizing Hypergraph-Based RAG:
Toward Better Fact Extraction and Chunk Retrieval"
([arXiv:2607.20506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20506), 2 Jul 2026; APIA 2026) states the problem
plainly: HyperGraphRAG "relies on error-prone LLM extraction and inefficient standard chunk
retrieval," and proposes self-consistency prompting plus Personalized PageRank retrieval as
mitigations. Related 2026 work describes "structural instabilities such as omitted entities or
isolated hyperedges, and deficient coreference resolution, which fragment the graph topology"
(see [HyperSU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28351), 3 Jun 2026). The construction-quality problem is
treated in [../03-construction/entity-resolution-and-canonicalisation.md](../03-construction/entity-resolution-and-canonicalisation.md).

**Net reading as of 2026-09.** There is good evidence that hyperedge-shaped representations *retain
more of a document's factual structure* than binary triples. There is much weaker evidence that this
translates into end-to-end answer quality gains that survive a fair baseline, a fair retrieval
budget, and a corpus that was not chosen to favour structure. Headline percentages in this
literature are usually against another *graph* system, not against a well-tuned vanilla retriever.
See also [../10-comparative-and-critique/critical-reading-of-hypergraph-rag-claims.md](../10-comparative-and-critique/critical-reading-of-hypergraph-rag-claims.md).

## 6. 2026: consolidation on four fronts

### 6.1 Construction gets a benchmark

Huang, Feng, Xue, Ying, Yong, Shi, Du, Gao, "Hyper-KGGen: A Skill-Driven Knowledge Extractor for
High-Quality Knowledge Hypergraph Generation" ([arXiv:2602.19543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19543),
23 Feb 2026): coarse-to-fine document decomposition, an adaptive "Global Skill Library" that
accumulates domain expertise, and — the durable contribution — **HyperDocRED**, an annotated
document-level knowledge-hypergraph benchmark. Until 2026 the construction side had no shared
ground truth at all.

### 6.2 Retrieval gets more structured

- **HyperRAG** — Lien, Chan, Hsiao, Ruan, Chiang, Chen, Yeh, Shuai, "HyperRAG: Reasoning N-ary Facts
  over Hypergraphs for Retrieval Augmented Generation"
  ([arXiv:2602.14470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14470), 16 Feb 2026; **WWW 2026**): builds
  query-conditioned relational chains over hyperedges for interpretable multi-hop reasoning,
  plus a memory component that uses the model's parametric knowledge to guide beam search.
- **HyperSU** — Liu et al. ([arXiv:2606.28351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28351), 3 Jun 2026):
  treats hyperedge formation as an entity-aware *minimum-description-length* optimisation, which is
  the first principled answer to "what should count as one hyperedge"; reports up to 14.7% relative
  accuracy improvement on GraphRAG-Bench.
- **HyperGraphPro** — Park, Lee, Khan, Kim, Kim
  ([arXiv:2601.17755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17755), 25 Jan 2026): reinforcement learning with
  progress-aware dense rewards for traversal over hypergraph-structured knowledge.

### 6.3 Hypergraphs become agent memory

This is the newest use, and it is not RAG: the hypergraph is written *by* the agent, *during* the
task, as working or skill memory.

- **HyperMem** — Yue, Hu, Sheng, Zhou, Zhang, Liu, Guo, Deng, "HyperMem: Hypergraph Memory for
  Long-Term Conversations" ([arXiv:2604.08256](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08256), 9 Apr 2026;
  **ACL 2026 Main**, [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1627/)): memory
  structured as topics → episodes → facts, with hyperedges grouping related episodes and their
  facts; 92.73% LLM-as-a-judge accuracy on the LoCoMo benchmark.
- **DocTrace** — Zai, Tan, Chen, Wang, Zhang, "Trace Only What You Need: Structure-Aware On-Demand
  Hypergraph Memory for Long-Document Question Answering"
  ([arXiv:2606.10921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10921), 9 Jun 2026): the hypergraph is built
  *on demand during reasoning* rather than indexed in advance — a direct answer to the cost
  objection against graph RAG.
- **HyperSkill** — Xu, Yang, Huang, "HyperSkill: Self-Evolving LLM Agents via Hypergraph-Structured
  Skill Memory" ([arXiv:2608.16114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16114), 17 Aug 2026): hyperedges link
  the subtasks and reusable skills of one trajectory; dual-path retrieval and quality-weighted
  hypergraph propagation for maintenance.

### 6.4 The hypergraph becomes a token sequence

Lei, Xie, Ying, Du, Yong, Shi, Tian, Li, Gao, "Hypergraph as Language"
([arXiv:2605.21858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21858), 21 May 2026; revised 15 Aug 2026). Hyper-Align
serialises hypergraphs for language models with a "Hypergraph Incidence Detail Template with
Overview" and projects incidence structure into the model's token space; ships HyperAlign-Bench.

The multimodal counterpart is Wei et al., "HyperGVL: Benchmarking and Improving Large Vision-Language
Models in Hypergraph Understanding and Reasoning"
([arXiv:2604.15648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15648), 17 Apr 2026, under review): 84,000 QA samples
over 12 tasks, 12 models, and — most useful for practitioners — a comparison of **12 textual and
visual hypergraph representations**, i.e. an empirical answer to "how should I show a hypergraph to a
model." See also [../06-visualization/visual-encodings-catalogue.md](../06-visualization/visual-encodings-catalogue.md).

And the agentic-multimodal combination: Lin et al., "EvoGraph-R1: Self-Evolving Multimodal Knowledge
Hypergraphs for Agentic Retrieval" ([arXiv:2607.12764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12764),
14 Jul 2026; **CVPR 2026**), which treats the hypergraph as a *dynamic environment* an agent acts on
(query, expand by web search, refine structure, answer) rather than a fixed index.

## 7. Hype versus shown, as of 2026-09

**Shown, with reasonable confidence:**

- Language models can extract n-ary/hyperedge-shaped facts from text at useful quality, and the
  hyperedge form loses less of a sentence's structure than a triple decomposition
  (HyperGraphRAG, Hyper-KGGen/HyperDocRED).
- Hypergraph-shaped retrieval indexes give measurable gains over binary graph RAG systems on
  multi-hop and domain QA benchmarks (HyperGraphRAG, Hyper-RAG, PRoH, HyperSU).
- Transfer across arities and across unseen relation vocabularies is possible (HYPER).
- Hypergraph memory helps long-conversation and long-document agents on their own benchmarks
  (HyperMem, DocTrace, HyperSkill).

**Claimed but not established:**

- That hypergraph RAG beats a well-tuned *vanilla* retriever in general, rather than beating graph
  RAG on structure-favouring corpora (the GraphRAG-Bench finding cuts the other way).
- That a hypergraph "reduces hallucination" as a mechanism rather than as a measured outcome on one
  domain.
- That scaling laws for hypergraph foundation models hold beyond the single curated corpus they were
  measured on.
- That the construction step is stable enough for production: the 2026 extraction critiques say
  otherwise.

**Largely absent from the literature:** cost and latency accounting for building and maintaining the
hypergraph; incremental update; provenance and retraction; and any head-to-head evaluation against
an ordinary relational database holding the same n-ary facts.
