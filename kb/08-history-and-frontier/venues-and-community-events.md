# Venues and community events

Where knowledge-hypergraph work is published and discussed, and the annual rhythm it follows. All
dates checked on **2026-09-20**; conference sites change, so re-check before planning around one.

The single most useful thing to know: **there is no knowledge-hypergraph conference.** The work is
distributed across four venue families that correspond to the four communities of
[machine-learning-era.md](machine-learning-era.md), and the only places they physically meet are
NetSci satellites, Dagstuhl seminars and a handful of large-ML workshops.

## 1. The four venue families

| Family | Venues | What appears there |
|---|---|---|
| **Semantic Web / KG** | ISWC, ESWC, The Web Conference (WWW) | Hyper-relational KGs, qualifiers, RDF-star/RDF 1.2, KHG-RAG systems |
| **Data mining / DB** | KDD, CIKM, SIGMOD, VLDB, ICDE | Hypergraph mining, HGNN surveys and tutorials, graph/hypergraph databases |
| **Machine learning** | NeurIPS, ICLR, ICML, AAAI, IJCAI, LoG | Embeddings, HGNNs, foundation models, expressivity theory |
| **NLP** | ACL, EMNLP, NAACL | Extraction of n-ary facts, hypergraph memory, hypergraph-RAG |
| **Network science / physics** | NetSci (+ satellites), CompleNet, *Physics Reports*, *Nature Physics*, *SIAM Review* | Higher-order structure and dynamics, libraries, datasets |
| **Visualization** | IEEE VIS, EuroVis, Graph Drawing | Hypergraph drawing, set visualisation, readability studies |

## 2. Semantic Web and Web venues

- **ISWC — International Semantic Web Conference.** ISWC 2026 is the **25th** edition,
  **25–29 October 2026, Bari, Italy** ([iswc2026.semanticweb.org](https://iswc2026.semanticweb.org/)).
  Historically the venue for reification, n-ary modelling and RDF-star work; Ali, Berrendorf, Galkin
  et al.'s hyper-relational inductive link prediction paper was an ISWC 2021 best paper
  ([arXiv:2107.04894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04894)).
- **ESWC — European Semantic Web Conference.** ESWC 2026 is the **23rd** edition,
  **10–14 May 2026, Dubrovnik, Croatia**
  ([2026.eswc-conferences.org](https://2026.eswc-conferences.org/calls/contributions-eswc/)).
- **WWW / The Web Conference.** Where the two n-ary formalisations were published: NaLP
  ([WWW 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3308558.3313414)) and HINGE
  ([WWW 2020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3366423.3380257)). Still active: PRoH and HyperRAG were both
  accepted to **WWW 2026** ([arXiv:2510.12434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12434);
  [arXiv:2602.14470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14470)).

## 3. Data mining and database venues

- **KDD.** Hosts the field's tutorial-style syntheses: the HGNN survey appeared at KDD 2024,
  pp. 6534–6544 ([DOI 10.1145/3637528.3671457](https://doi.org/10.1145/3637528.3671457)).
- **CIKM.** The critical-evaluation venue in practice: Wang et al.'s "Understanding the Embedding
  Models on Hyper-relational Knowledge Graph" was CIKM 2025
  ([arXiv:2508.03280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03280)), and a hypergraph-neural-network **tutorial**
  ran at CIKM 2025, pp. 6829–6832
  ([DOI 10.1145/3746252.3761449](https://doi.org/10.1145/3746252.3761449)).
- **SIGMOD / PODS / VLDB / ICDE.** Where the standards-adjacent work lands: PG-Schema at SIGMOD 2023
  ([DOI 10.1145/3589778](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589778)). Higher-order graph databases are so far
  a preprint ([arXiv:2506.19661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19661)), which suggests the DB community
  has not yet absorbed the topic.

## 4. Machine-learning venues

- **NeurIPS.** HyperGraphRAG was accepted to the **NeurIPS 2025 main conference**
  ([proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/df55ee6e59f8ac4a625219e11fe9ddba-Paper-Conference.pdf)).
  NeurIPS workshops matter as much: HYPER was presented at the NeurIPS 2025 **"New Perspectives in
  Graph Machine Learning"** workshop ([page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/127653)).
- **ICLR.** ULTRA (ICLR 2024), GraphRAG-Bench (ICLR 2026,
  [arXiv:2506.05690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05690)), and HYPER per its authors' repository
  ([GitHub](https://github.com/HxyScotthuang/HYPER)).
- **ICML.** The topological-deep-learning position paper, ICML 2024, PMLR 235
  ([arXiv:2402.08871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08871)). ICML 2026 ran a **"Graph Foundation Models:
  A New Era for Graph Machine Learning"** workshop on Friday 10 July 2026
  ([Google at ICML 2026](https://research.google/conferences-and-events/google-at-icml-2026/)).
- **AAAI / IJCAI.** The historical anchors: HGNN at AAAI 2019
  ([DOI 10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33013558](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33013558)); m-TransH at
  IJCAI 2016 and "Knowledge Hypergraphs" at IJCAI 2020
  ([DOI 10.24963/ijcai.2020/303](https://doi.org/10.24963/ijcai.2020/303)).
- **LoG — Learning on Graphs Conference.** The community's own venue, founded 2022. **LoG 2026,
  20–22 November 2026, Northeastern University, Boston** — the fifth edition overall and the second
  in person ([logconference.org](https://logconference.org/)). Previous editions 2022, 2023, 2024,
  2025. This is the best single place to see graph *and* hypergraph learning together.
- **CVPR.** Now appearing, via multimodal agentic retrieval: EvoGraph-R1, CVPR 2026
  ([arXiv:2607.12764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12764)).

## 5. NLP venues

- **ACL.** HyperMem, "Hypergraph Memory for Long-Term Conversations," **ACL 2026 Main**
  ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1627/)).
- **EMNLP.** StarE, EMNLP 2020 ([anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.596/)), and
  the field survey: Wei, Guan et al., "A Survey of Link Prediction in N-ary Knowledge Graphs,"
  EMNLP 2025 main, pp. 28545–28567 ([anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1451/)).

Note the pattern: **the NLP venues took the topic seriously only once LLMs were involved.** Before
2024 the n-ary work sat in WWW/IJCAI; after 2024 it also sits in ACL/EMNLP.

## 6. Network science: NetSci and its satellites

This is where the higher-order community physically gathers, and it is the most reliable annual
rhythm in the field.

**NetSci 2026 — 1–5 June 2026, Hyatt Regency Boston Cambridge, Boston, MA**, hosted by Northeastern
University's Network Science Institute; the **20th anniversary** edition of the series
([netsci2026.com](https://www.netsci2026.com/)). Satellite workshops run 1–2 June
([satellites page](https://www.netsci2026.com/satellites)), with 24+ satellites in total.

The hypergraph-relevant ones:

- **HONS — Higher-Order Network Science.** A recurring NetSci satellite; the 2026 edition is a
  half-day on the morning of **1 June 2026**, themed on how higher-order models can support
  *interpretability* in machine-learning applications. Previous editions have been held in Berkeley,
  Zaragoza, Seoul, Indianapolis, Paris and Burlington, Vermont
  ([hons-web.github.io](https://hons-web.github.io/online/); an earlier edition site:
  [HONS 2020](https://uzhdag.github.io/hons_web/)).
- **TopoNets 2026 — 2 June 2026, Boston.** "Networks beyond pairwise interactions": structural
  measures, generative models, emergent phenomena. Organisers: Jesseba Fernando (Northeastern
  University), Tim LaRock (Princeton University), Maxime Lucas (University of Namur), Federico
  Malizia (Central European University), Marco Nurisso (Polytechnic of Turin). The site lists nine
  prior editions (TopoNets 14, 15, GeoTopoNets 16, TopoNets 20–25)
  ([TopoNets 2026](https://sites.google.com/view/toponets2026/home-page)).
- **A Higher Order Hackathon — "A Blue Start," Sunday 31 May 2026**, Renaissance Park, Northeastern
  University, Boston: a one-day pre-conference hackathon on a Bluesky starter-pack dataset, ending
  in lightning talks at the conference. Co-organised by Phil Chodrow with Alyssa Smith and Nicholas
  Landry ([hackathon page](https://www.philchodrow.prof/higher-order-hackathon/);
  [Chodrow's site](https://www.philchodrow.prof/)).

The hackathon format is worth noting as an institution: it is where the library maintainers
(XGI, HyperNetX, Hypergraphx) get people using the tools on a common dataset, and it is one of the
forces behind the HIF interchange format
([standards-convergence.md](standards-convergence.md)).

## 7. Visualization venues

- **IEEE VIS.** The premier venue for visualization and visual analytics; **VIS 2026, 9–13 November
  2026, Back Bay, Boston, Massachusetts** ([ieeevis.org](https://ieeevis.org/year/2026/welcome)).
  Hypergraph-relevant work appears mostly as *set visualisation* (Euler/Venn-style, matrix-based and
  bipartite encodings) rather than under the word "hypergraph"; see
  [../06-visualization/visual-encodings-catalogue.md](../06-visualization/visual-encodings-catalogue.md).
- **Graph Drawing (GD) — International Symposium on Graph Drawing and Network Visualization.**
  Annual since **1992** (first edition in Marino, near Rome, organised by Giuseppe Di Battista,
  Peter Eades, Pierre Rosenstiehl and Roberto Tamassia); scope covers graph drawing, network
  information visualization and geometric graph theory. Proceedings were published by Springer from
  1994 to 2023 and by **LIPIcs from 2024**
  ([Wikipedia, International Symposium on Graph Drawing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Symposium_on_Graph_Drawing)).
  The 2026 edition's dates and location were not verified in this run `[unverified]`.

## 8. Dagstuhl seminars

Schloss Dagstuhl seminars are the closest thing the field has to a cross-community summit.

- **Dagstuhl Seminar 21352, "Higher-Order Graph Models: From Theoretical Foundations to Machine
  Learning," 29 August – 1 September 2021**, organised by Tina Eliassi-Rad, Vito Latora, Martin
  Rosvall and Ingo Scholtes. Scope: generalising network analysis to models capturing more than
  dyadic relations — hypergraphs, simplicial models, high-dimensional De Bruijn graphs, multilayer
  networks ([Dagstuhl 21352](https://www.dagstuhl.de/seminars/seminar-calendar/seminar-details/21352)).

No later Dagstuhl seminar dedicated to hypergraphs or higher-order models was found in this run
`[unverified]`; hypergraphs appear as a *topic* inside seminars on other subjects (parameterized
algorithms, graph decomposition, SAT). The seminar calendar is at
[dagstuhl.de/en/seminars/seminar-calendar](https://www.dagstuhl.de/en/seminars/seminar-calendar),
and proposals are called for annually
([call for proposals, deadline 15 April 2026](https://www.dagstuhl.de/en/institute/news/2026/cfp-0626)).

**This is a gap worth noting**: the knowledge-hypergraph community (KG embedding + LLM systems) has
never had a Dagstuhl of its own, and the 2021 seminar was network-science-led.

## 9. The annual rhythm

A rough calendar for someone tracking the field:

| Period | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Jan–Feb** | ICLR decisions; WWW decisions; a burst of arXiv preprints positioning for the spring conferences |
| **Mar–Apr** | ICLR (spring); ESWC (May); ACL/IJCAI deadlines; W3C and ISO document snapshots have tended to land in April (GQL 12 Apr 2024; RDF 1.2 CR 7 Apr 2026) |
| **May–Jun** | WWW; **NetSci and its higher-order satellites** (the community's one physical meeting point); ICML |
| **Jul–Aug** | ICML; KDD; ACL; the summer preprint wave |
| **Sep–Oct** | NeurIPS decisions; EMNLP; CIKM; **ISWC**; ICLR submissions |
| **Nov–Dec** | **IEEE VIS**; **LoG**; NeurIPS; journal versions of the year's conference papers appear |

Two structural observations. First, the **arXiv preprint is the real publication event** in this
field: the median gap between preprint and archival venue in the papers surveyed here is several
months to a year, and several influential items (Hyper-FM, Hyper-RAG, HYPER) were widely cited long
before the journal or conference version. Second, **journal versions matter for the network-science
and learning-theory strands** (*Network Science*, *Journal of Complex Networks*, *TPAMI*, *JMLR*,
*ACM CSUR*) and barely at all for the LLM-systems strand.

## 10. Where to look for an entry point

- To **meet people**: NetSci satellites (HONS, TopoNets) and the higher-order hackathon; LoG.
- To **read the state of the art**: NeurIPS/ICLR/WWW for systems, *ACM Computing Surveys* for
  synthesis, *Network Science* and *Journal of Complex Networks* for structure and data.
- To **follow standards**: the W3C RDF & SPARQL Working Group publications page and the ISO/IEC
  JTC1 SC32 catalogue entries — see [standards-convergence.md](standards-convergence.md).
- To **track software**: the library repositories listed in
  [research-groups-and-people.md](research-groups-and-people.md) and `kb/09-ecosystem/`.
