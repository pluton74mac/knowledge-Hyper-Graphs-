# Chemistry and reaction networks

Chemistry is the purest example of sense **C** in [applications-overview](applications-overview.md):
a hyperedge is a *directed transformation* from a multiset of reactants to a multiset of products. No
one in this field argues about whether the hypergraph is the right model; the arguments are about which
algorithms to run on it. That makes chemistry a useful control case for the rest of this KB — it shows
what a mature hypergraph application looks like when the n-ary structure is not in dispute.

## 1. The formalism

A chemical reaction network is a **directed multi-hypergraph**: vertices are molecules, hyperedges are
reactions, and each hyperedge carries stoichiometric coefficients on its tail and head
([Andersen, Flamm, Merkle, Stadler, 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02594)). "Multi-" matters because
the same reactant may appear with coefficient > 1, so tails and heads are multisets, not sets. This is
the same structure that [Klamt, Haus and Theis, 2009](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000385)
use for metabolism — see [biomedical-and-life-sciences](biomedical-and-life-sciences.md) — and it
specialises the general directed hypergraph of
[Gallo, Longo, Pallottino and Nguyen, 1993](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(93)90045-P).

Two consequences follow immediately and are not available in a graph model:

- **A pathway is a flow, not a path.** Andersen et al. model pathways as *integer hyperflows* with
  routing constraints, so a pathway is a set of reactions with integer stoichiometric coefficients that
  balances. Because the flows must be integral, finding them means solving integer linear programs,
  which they accept in exchange for mechanistic precision that flux balance analysis and elementary
  mode analysis do not give. They demonstrate on non-oxidative glycolysis (improving hand-designed
  pathways) and on the formose process, with the networks themselves generated by graph transformation
  ([Andersen et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02594)).
- **Querying by motif.** Because the pathway is an object in the same algebra as the network, one can
  query a network for general "chemical transformation motifs" and enumerate optimal and near-optimal
  pathways automatically.

The line continues: pathway *realizability* in chemical networks
([Andersen, Banke, Fagerberg, Flamm, Merkle, Stadler, 2025](https://dx.doi.org/10.1089/cmb.2024.0521),
*Journal of Computational Biology*), and assembly theory recast in directed hypergraphs, where assembly
pathways are shown to coincide with certain minimal hyperpaths in B-hypergraphs, connecting assembly to
rule-based chemistry and DPO graph rewriting
([*Assembly in Directed Hypergraphs*, Proceedings of the Royal Society A 481(2324):20250331, 2025](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspa/article/481/2324/20250331/234367/Assembly-in-Directed-HypergraphsAssembly-in)).

Finding pathways under energy-barrier constraints by integer linear programming over the same
hypergraph is a 2026 continuation ([Pal et al., 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/minf.70021),
*Molecular Informatics*; preprint [arXiv:2504.10609](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10609v1)).

## 2. Retrosynthesis: AND/OR trees are hypergraphs

Retrosynthetic search is the best-known place where chemists meet directed hypergraphs without always
naming them. The retrosynthesis space is an **AND/OR graph**: an OR node is a molecule (several
reactions could make it), an AND node is a reaction (all its reactants are required). An AND/OR graph is
exactly a directed hypergraph, which is one of the applications Gallo et al. list in 1993
([Gallo et al., 1993](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(93)90045-P)).

Concretely:

- [Schwaller et al., 2020](https://pubs.rsc.org/sc/article/11/12/3316/664127/Predicting-retrosynthetic-pathways-using)
  (*Chemical Science* 11:3316) combine a transformer single-step model with a "hyper-graph exploration
  strategy", noting that the retrosynthesis graph "could also be considered as a hypergraph where an
  edge can connect several nodes, thus representing the link between products and substrates"
  (preprint [arXiv:1910.08036](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.08036)).
- **RetroSiG** ([Retrosynthesis Prediction via Search in (Hyper) Graph, 2024](https://openreview.net/forum?id=igUP5kQRij);
  [arXiv:2402.06772](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240206772L/abstract)) reformulates
  semi-template retrosynthesis as two search problems: reaction-centre identification as search in the
  product molecular graph, and leaving-group completion as search in a *leaving-group hypergraph*.
- Bi-level Monte Carlo tree search over a hypergraph — where hypernodes represent a parent molecule and
  hyperactions represent reactions involving several molecules at that level — appears in the patent
  literature ([US 11,354,582 B1, *System and method for automated retrosynthesis*](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11354582)),
  which is a useful signal that the formalism has left academia.

The practical reason the hypergraph view matters in retrosynthesis is cost accounting: a route is
complete only when *every* leaf is purchasable, so the search must propagate an AND over sibling
reactants. Systems that flatten to a tree of molecules have to reimplement that AND somewhere.

## 3. What chemistry does *not* give the KHG story

It is worth being precise about the limits of the analogy.

- Chemistry hyperedges have **no roles and no qualifiers** in the knowledge-representation sense — they
  have stoichiometry and direction. A reaction hypergraph is not a knowledge hypergraph in the sense of
  [what-is-a-knowledge-hypergraph](../02-knowledge-representation/what-is-a-knowledge-hypergraph.md);
  it is a computational model of a process.
- The payoff is algorithmic (flows, hyperpaths, B-connectivity, motif queries), not representational.
  Nobody builds a reaction hypergraph in order to *retrieve* facts about it with an LLM.
- Conversely, chemistry supplies the algorithms that the knowledge-hypergraph side mostly has not
  adopted: integer hyperflows, minimal hyperpaths, B-connectivity. Transferring those to knowledge
  hypergraphs is an open exploration vector.
