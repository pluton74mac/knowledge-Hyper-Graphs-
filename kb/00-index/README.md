## The ten sections in one paragraph each

**01 Foundations.** A hypergraph is a set of vertices with a family of vertex subsets. There is no canonical matrix for it; every Laplacian in common use reduces to a clique or star expansion, and clique expansion is provably non-invertible. The bipartite (Levi) graph is lossless only if the two sides are labelled. A simplicial complex adds downward closure, an assertion that is false for n-ary facts. Fagin's acyclicity hierarchy and hypertree width govern query tractability; the AGM bound says the arity structure, not the data, sets the worst case.

**02 Knowledge representation.** "Knowledge hypergraph" has no agreed definition. Three fact formalisations compete: positional hyperedge, unordered role-value set, primary triple plus qualifiers. Reification is lossless but embedding-hostile; star-to-clique is lossy. The most-used benchmark leaks. Standards give statement identity but no first-class n-ary predicate. A ground relational database already is a labelled ordered knowledge hypergraph.

**03 Construction.** A schema is a type signature: relation, arity, role per argument. LLM pipelines converge on conceptualise, structure, canonicalise. Incremental construction is solved only for append. Evaluation is the weakest link: strict exact-match F1, one document-level benchmark, no arity-stratified results. Curation still supplies almost all real n-ary facts.

**04 Storage and formats.** HIF is the only cross-library hypergraph format with a schema and several implementations, but it has no version field and no role vocabulary. GraphML supports hyperedges in the spec and not in the tooling. RDF 1.2 and the older RDF-star dialect are incompatible in the field. The flagship hypergraph-RAG system stores a bipartite graph. No distributed transactional native hypergraph database exists; use an incidence table as system of record.

**05 Query, embeddings, reasoning.** No standard KHG query language exists; TypeQL is the only deployed language with native n-ary patterns. Hypertree width bounds both query evaluation and hypergraph-neural-network expressivity. Embedding architectures converged on attention; remaining gains are structural. Foundation models and expressivity theory arrived in 2025 to 2026. Explainability and uncertainty are nearly empty.

**06 Visualization.** Every knowledge system draws n-ary facts as fact nodes with role-labelled links; the region-based encodings favoured by academia are NP-hard to lay out and collapse at six to eight sets. PAOH is the reference temporal encoding and the only one with a role channel. No maintained, web-native, role-aware hypergraph viewer exists.

**07 Applications.** Hypergraph RAG is the busiest line, with gains that are larger on binary than on n-ary questions and no entry in the one neutral benchmark. The best-evidenced results are outside the LLM literature: metabolic B-connectivity, adverse-event records, fraud detection, dependency resolution. Industry is mostly binary; directed hypergraphs are ubiquitous and unnamed in build systems and planners.

**08 History and frontier.** From Berge around 1960 through semantic networks, Freebase compound values, Wikidata qualifiers, the 2016 to 2021 embedding wave, the 2019 hypergraph-neural-network wave, and the 2025 RAG wave. Five structural reasons explain why n-ary knowledge kept being flattened. The most important 2025 to 2026 development is negative: neutral benchmarks find graph RAG often underperforms plain retrieval.

**09 Ecosystem.** Network-science libraries and knowledge libraries barely overlap; none exposes incidence roles. HIF round-trips are not lossless across libraries. rdflib cannot parse RDF 1.2. Adoption follows packaging. Hypergraph-RAG corpora are not archival.

**10 Comparative and critique.** The bipartite objection is correct and conceded; what survives is bicolouring, non-Markovian walks, and statistics. The motivating "one third of facts are n-ary" statistic is misread. Two hypergraph communities do not read each other. The critical question is whether any result holds of knowledge hypergraphs that does not hold of arbitrary sets of ground atoms.
