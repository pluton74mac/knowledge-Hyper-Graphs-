# Research run 02 — second pass seeded by an external frontier survey

## Brief from the repository owner

A one-shot research run on another harness produced a ten-section survey of the knowledge-hypergraph
frontier (foundation models, hypergraph RAG, extraction, representation, agent memory, theory,
evaluation, a composed stack, ranked bets, and a reading path). The brief: treat it as a seed for a
second research pass, check what the base had not touched, and update or create the relevant notes.
All research agents were to run on the Opus 5 tier, in parallel.

## Method

1. Grepped `kb/` for every system, number and claim in the seed. Most of it was already covered
   (HYPER, PRoH, Hyper-KGGen, HyperMem, HyperSkill, EvoGraph-R1, Hyper-FM, ReAlE, the two-axis
   taxonomy). Untouched or thin: HYPER's internals and cost, KG-ICL, H²RAG and HiRAG, H²GNN and
   hyperbolic geometry, ReAlE as a planner interface, Hyper-KGGen's skill library read against its
   code, Hyper-Extract's actual feature set, valid time on the hyperedge, and the seed's own synthesis
   (the composed stack, the four abilities that evaluation conflates, the seven bets).
2. Launched five parallel research agents, each with a narrow brief, an explicit list of files it
   could edit, and an append-only convention for the by-topic source files. Each agent verified the
   seed's claims against primary sources (arXiv HTML by version, OpenReview, Springer, ACM, GitHub
   raw files, PyPI) and reported findings, README rows, open questions and glossary entries back to
   the orchestrator rather than editing index files.
3. The orchestrator wrote the synthesis note, integrated the five reports (renumbering colliding
   question ids), applied the cross-cutting corrections the agents flagged in files outside their
   scope, regenerated the map of content and bibliography, and validated.

## What the seed got right, and what it got wrong

The seed's structural reading held up: the field's question has moved from embedding n-ary facts to
building one system that extracts, stores, completes, plans over and remembers them, and no published
system implements more than two or three of those layers. Its specific claims fared unevenly.

**Confirmed from primary sources.** HYPER's relation graph, `Enc_PI`, two HCNet passes, slot-masked
training, and the 3KG+2HG > 50KG mixture result; HCNet's no-inverse-augmentation result (sharper than
stated: NBFNet-style models *need* augmentation to match it); Hyper-FM's diversity-over-volume
scaling law; PRoH's planning, sub-question DAG and EWO; Hyper-KGGen's coarse-to-fine ladder and
skill library; HyperMem's 92.73% LoCoMo score; HyperSkill's benchmarks (xBench, GAIA,
WebWalkerQA); EvoGraph-R1's action set; H²RAG's existence (PAKDD 2026); H²GNN's existence; the
two-axis taxonomy's axes; ReAlE's five primitives; the graph-based agent-memory survey listing
hypergraphs as first-class storage.

**Corrected.**
- HYPER's venue is settled: ICLR 2026 (OpenReview PDF header and repository BibTeX). The base's
  hedge was removed from seven files.
- The base's HYPER result tables were the v1 preprint's; v3 changed the reified-ULTRA baselines by
  up to a factor of 50 and reversed the headline wording from "marginally better" to "much worse".
- HYPER's cost is 1.7× HCNet and 3.8× ULTRA in time but only 1.4× and 2.0× in memory; the seed's
  "2× and 4× in time and memory" doubled the memory factor. Theorem 4.1 is existential (formal
  version C.2) and says nothing about the trained encoder.
- PRoH's +19.73 is F1 *points*, not per cent; its "harder" 3–6 hop split is self-generated and both
  systems score higher on it; token costs are reported only for the lightweight variant.
- ReAlE's fifth primitive is set difference, not join; Cartesian product is the one Codd primitive it
  does not claim. Two base files said "join". ReAlE's venue is JMLR 24(105), 2023.
- The seed's "role-aware methods are thin, position-aware dominates" is wrong by headcount (23
  role-aware vs 17 position-aware in the taxonomy's table); the defensible statements are that
  "role-aware" there means qualifier-aware, that every geometry and foundation model is
  position-aware, and that no model uses a linguistic role inventory.
- H²GNN's headline does not survive its own tables: its FB-AUTO MRR 0.757 is below HypE's published
  0.804 and ReAlE's 0.861; it is a preprint with no venue or code.
- The seed's "n-ary extrapolation has not been done" is wrong: NE-Net, MT-Path and HTKGH do it. What
  is absent is n-ary *valid time, invalidation and supersession*.
- Hyper-KGGen is a KDD 2026 paper; HyperDocRED is announced but not distributed; the "top-heavy
  extraction" failure mode is asserted, not measured; skill acquisition is supervised against gold
  and only partly implemented in the released code.
- Hyper-Extract is Apache-2.0 (not "Other"), has ten engines and 40 templates (not "11+" and "80+"),
  and its hyperedge identity key excludes participants, so an insert can silently promote a binary
  edge to n-ary.

**New to the base.** Graph-R1 (ICML 2026, from the HyperGraphRAG authors: average F1 57.82 against
HyperGraphRAG's 29.40, the field's clearest internal evidence that search matters more than
representation); HyperGraphPro (retitled from ProGraph-R1); HiRAG's index cost (about 35 LLM tokens
per corpus token); HHS-RAG, EEG-MedRAG, ArchRAG, EHRAG; KG-ICL as a measured baseline; MT-Path,
HTKGH, EdgeMem, MAGE, Quipu and the graph-native bitemporal store; GAHE; LOTUS semantic operators as
the nearest thing to an algebraic planner interface; HYPER's public checkpoints, in-repo datasets and
Triton kernel; the n-ary pretraining corpus ceiling (about 0.91 M facts, about 90 k at arity ≥ 3).

## Dated absence claims (all 2026-09-21)

- No paper combines inductive hypergraph completion with retrieval.
- No hypergraph-RAG paper plots accuracy against a token budget.
- No hypergraph memory system adopts a bitemporal model; no experiment isolates supersession for one.
- No continuous-time n-ary temporal model; no temporal complex query answering over hyper-relational
  graphs.
- No consistency guarantee for incremental knowledge-hypergraph extraction.
- No δ-hyperbolicity statistic for any n-ary benchmark; no hyperbolic model combined with a
  relation graph.
- No n-ary model using a linguistic role inventory; no planner exposing relational-algebra operators
  over an n-ary store.
- No independent replication of HYPER or of reified ULTRA on its 16 datasets; no n-ary pretraining
  corpus larger than HYPER's four.
