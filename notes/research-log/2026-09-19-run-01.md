# Research run 01 — building the initial knowledge base

## Brief from the repository owner

Questions asked: What is a knowledge hypergraph? How are they constructed? How are they stored, in
which file types? What are the applications? How are they visualised? Expand beyond these questions.
The base must include development history and the current research frontier. Finish with open
questions for further exploration.

## Method

1. Designed the section layout in `CONVENTIONS.md` (ten numbered `kb/` sections, plus `sources/`,
   `projects/`, `schemas/`, `datasets/`, `tools/`).
2. Ran ten parallel research agents, one per section, each doing web research on primary sources
   (papers, specifications, repositories, documentation) and writing cited notes into its section only.
3. Reviewed the output for gaps and contradictions, ran a second pass on gaps.
4. Wrote the cross-cutting documents: top-level `README.md`, `kb/00-index/` (map of content, glossary,
   reading paths, open questions), merged bibliography.
5. Validated with `tools/validate_kb.py`.

## What happened

The run spanned 2026-09-19 and 2026-09-20. The first wave of ten agents was cut off twice by session
rate limits; each time the partial notes were committed and the agents were resumed or relaunched
with instructions to build on what existed. From the second relaunch onward every agent ran on the
same model tier at the repository owner's request. Every agent exhausted its web-search budget
before finishing and completed its research through direct fetches of arXiv, Crossref, OpenAlex,
DBLP, GitHub pages and specification sites.

A second pass ran three agents: a cross-section consistency and fact-check reviewer (22 contradictions
resolved across 39 files, logged in `2026-09-20-consistency-review.md`), a glossary builder (304
entries), and a gap-filler that wrote six notes on the topics every section had flagged as thin
(multimodal and multilingual construction, annotation cost and human factors, courses and
workshops, privacy and licensing, reproducibility of n-ary link prediction, and a dated snapshot of
June to September 2026 arXiv output). A final small pass marked the two senses of "reification",
the three senses of "directed hypergraph", and the KB's arity convention wherever they were used
unmarked.

## Ten findings that reshape the questions asked

1. "Knowledge hypergraph" has no agreed definition. Three fact formalisations compete (positional
   hyperedge, role-value set, triple plus qualifiers) and their benchmark scores are not comparable.
2. Every hypergraph is a bicoloured bipartite graph and the field concedes it; what survives the
   objection is bicolouring, non-Markovian s-walks, statistical null models, and the AGM bound.
3. The motivating "more than one third" statistic is about entities, not facts; fact-level shares are
   11.6% (WikiPeople, raw) and 13.6% (WD50K); the one dataset with 45.9% is the one that leaks.
4. Reification is information-preserving; reification-by-decomposition is what collapses learning;
   star-to-clique is the lossy conversion. Several papers cite the wrong one.
5. HIF is the only cross-library hypergraph format with a schema, but it has no version field, no
   role vocabulary, four schema URLs in circulation, and round-trips lose direction in two of three
   libraries. Nothing in the numeric or visual stack carries roles.
6. The flagship hypergraph-RAG system stores a bipartite graph; its gains are larger on binary-source
   questions than n-ary ones; the two flagship systems never cite each other; the one neutral
   benchmark contains no hypergraph system.
7. The best-evidenced applications are outside the LLM literature: metabolic B-connectivity,
   adverse-event records, fraud detection, package dependency resolution.
8. Both live standards re-affirmed binary edges in 2024 to 2026; standards converge on annotation,
   not arity. TypeDB is the only mainstream product with n-ary roles as a primitive.
9. The two hypergraph communities (higher-order network science; knowledge representation and
   learning) measurably do not cite each other.
10. No replication study, no arity-stratified result, no human-factors study, no calibration study,
    and no hypergraph-visualisation benchmark exist.

  the schema-file validation. All benchmark numbers are read from papers.

## Corrections to the original brief recorded in the notes

- Phil Chodrow is not an XGI core developer.
- DocTrace is multi-agent long-document question answering with hypergraph working memory, not
  repository-level code retrieval.
- The JF17K leakage figure is from Galkin et al. (StarE, EMNLP 2020), not Rosso et al. (HINGE).
- "XL-Event" could not be located from any primary source.
- HYPER's venue is ambiguous between a NeurIPS 2025 workshop and ICLR 2026.
